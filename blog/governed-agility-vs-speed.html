Why 'Governed Agility' Beats Pure Speed in Federal IT

The private sector mantra "move fast and break things" is a recipe for disaster in government. Here is how we reframe agility to deliver speed *with* auditability.

Eugene P. Nkomba Co-Managing Partner, New South Technologies

The Federal Fallacy

In Silicon Valley, velocity is the ultimate metric. If you aren't shipping daily, you're falling behind. The philosophy is simple: ship it, fix bugs later, and iterate based on user feedback.

In the federal space, this philosophy is not just wrong—it's dangerous.

If a consumer app breaks, users tweet about it. If a federal system breaks—say, the one processing unemployment benefits or Medicare claims—citizens lose access to essential services. If a federal system lacks proper governance, an Inspector General can shut down the entire program, freeze funding, and launch an investigation.

This reality creates a false dichotomy for many agency leaders: We must choose between Speed (Agile) or Safety (Governance).

That choice is a trap. Choosing safety means stagnation. Choosing speed means risking the mission.

Enter: Governed Agility

At New South Technologies, we reject this dichotomy. We propose a third path: Governed Agility.

Governed Agility is not "Agile but slower." It is a fundamentally different operating model where compliance is not a phase at the end of the project, but a continuous, automated output of the development process itself.

In a Governed Agility model:

  • Compliance is a Feature: Just like performance or security, it is built into the Definition of Done.
  • Audit Trails are Automatic: You don't write a Security Plan after the code is written; the pipeline generates the evidence as it builds the code.
  • Risk is Managed Continuously: You don't wait for an annual assessment; you run real-time control monitoring with every deployment.

How Governed Agility Works in Practice

Let's look at the difference between "Pure Agile" and "Governed Agility" in a typical federal scenario.

Scenario: Deploying a New Microservice

The Pure Agile Approach: The dev team builds the service, tests it locally, and deploys it to production on Friday afternoon because "it's done." *Result:* Two days later, a 3PAO finds the service has no logging enabled and violates data residency rules. The Authorizing Official (AO) issues a Findings of Non-Compliance. The service is taken offline. The team spends two weeks documenting what they did wrong. Velocity hits zero.

The Governed Agility Approach: The team works within a pipeline pre-configured with NIST 800-53 controls. 1. When code is committed, a static analysis tool checks for logging configurations. If missing, the build fails. 2. When the container is built, an image scanner checks for vulnerabilities. If high-severity issues exist, deployment is blocked. 3. Upon successful deployment, the pipeline automatically updates the System Security Plan (SSP) and logs the change in eMASS. *Result:* The service is deployed securely. The AO receives an automated notification that the new component is compliant. No manual paperwork. No shutdown.

The Four Pillars of Governed Agility

Implementing this framework requires shifting from four key areas:

  1. From "Check-the-Box" to "Code-as-Control": Instead of writing policies in Word documents that no one reads, we encode them in Infrastructure as Code (IaC). Terraform scripts become the policy. If the script passes, the control is met.
  2. From "Waterfall Audits" to "Continuous Monitoring": Traditional SA&A happens once every three years. Governed Agility uses tools like Cloud Splinting or Prisma Cloud to monitor controls 24/7. If a drift occurs, an alert is triggered immediately, not months later.
  3. From "Silos" to "Embedded Teams": In many agencies, developers, security engineers, and ISSOs work in separate rooms. We co-locate these roles. The ISSO sits in the Scrum ceremony. They are part of the team, not the police force waiting at the finish line.
  4. From "Velocity" to "Value": We stop measuring success by "story points completed" and start measuring by "controls satisfied per sprint." A feature isn't valuable if it can't be authorized.

The Business Case: Why This Wins Contracts

Federal CIOs are under immense pressure. They need modernization to keep up with citizen expectations, but they are terrified of OIG findings.

Vendors who promise "pure speed" often deliver chaos. Vendors who promise "governance" often deliver slowness.

Vendors who can promise Governed Agility—speed that is defensible and sustainable—are the ones winning the most critical contracts today. Why? Because they reduce the risk profile of the program. They make the CIO look good to their leadership. They turn the compliance headache into a competitive advantage.

The Bottom Line

Speed is easy. Anybody can write code and push it to a server. But in the federal space, speed without accountability is just debt waiting to be collected.

Governed Agility is the only way to modernize at scale without breaking the trust of the public or the scrutiny of the auditor. It's not about slowing down; it's about building the brakes *into* the engine so you can drive faster, safely, all the time.

That is the New South Technologies standard.
