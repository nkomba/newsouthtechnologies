5 Signs Your Agency's Legacy System Is a Compliance Risk

Manual compliance processes, siloed data, and undocumented interdependencies are red flags. Here is how to assess the risk before your OIG does.

Catherine A. Nkomba Managing Partner, New South Technologies

The Silent Threat in Your IT Portfolio

In almost every federal agency we speak with, there is one system everyone avoids talking about. The one that "just works," but no one remembers how it was built. The one where the original developers retired a decade ago. The one that runs on a server the Network Operations Center (NOC) is afraid to reboot.

This isn't just a technical debt problem. It is a compliance liability.

When an Inspector General (OIG) or a Third Party Assessor (3PAO) digs into these systems, they don't look at code efficiency. They look for evidence of control. If that evidence is manual, fragmented, or missing, the system fails its Authorization to Operate (ATO).

Here are the five tell-tale signs that your legacy system is a ticking time bomb.

Sign #1: Manual Evidence Collection

If your ISSO or security team spends weeks every year gathering screenshots, printing logs, and copying/pasting data into Word documents for the System Security Plan (SSP), you have a compliance risk.

Why it's a risk: Manual processes are prone to human error and fatigue. More importantly, they cannot keep pace with continuous monitoring requirements. If your controls are only checked once a year, you are technically non-compliant for the other 364 days. An OIG will flag this immediately as a failure of NIST SP 800-53 Control CA-7 (Continuous Monitoring).

The Fix: Automate evidence collection. Move from static documentation to dynamic, real-time compliance dashboards.

Sign #2: The "Siloed Data" Problem

Does your system store PII (Personally Identifiable Information) in a way that doesn't match your current data classification policies? For example, is unstructured data (emails, documents) sitting in a shared drive with CUI markings but without the required encryption or access controls?

Why it's a risk: Modern compliance frameworks like FedRAMP and CMMC require Data Loss Prevention (DLP) and strict data lifecycle management. If data flows out of your controlled legacy database into unmonitored spreadsheets or shadow IT storage, you have a data breach waiting to happen—and a violation of FISMA.

The Fix: Implement a unified data fabric strategy. Bring all data under a single governance policy, encrypt everything at rest and in transit, and remove silos.

Sign #3: Undocumented Interdependencies

Can you draw a complete architecture diagram of your system today? Do you know exactly what APIs call what databases, and which downstream systems rely on it? If the answer is "We think so" or "It's in someone's head," you are in danger.

Why it's a risk: When you change one part of a modern system, you need to know the impact on the whole. In a legacy system, a simple patch can break a critical business process because nobody knew two modules were linked. During an assessment, if you cannot demonstrate knowledge of your boundaries and interfaces, the assessor cannot validate your scope. This often leads to a Findings of Non-Compliance or a downgrade in your ATO status.

The Fix: Use automated discovery tools to map dependencies. Rebuild your architecture diagrams as living documents, not static PDFs.

Sign #4: Lack of Explainability

If your system makes decisions (even simple ones like "approve/deny" or "flag for review") based on rules that are hard-coded in spaghetti logic, you cannot explain *why* a decision was made.

Why it's a risk: With the rise of the NIST AI Risk Management Framework (and, for procured large language models, OMB M-26-04's unbiased-AI requirements), explainability is becoming an expectation even for non-AI systems. If a citizen asks why their benefits were denied, and your system has no log explaining the specific rule that triggered the denial, you are vulnerable to legal challenges and OIG scrutiny regarding fairness and due process.

The Fix: Refactor your business logic. Ensure every decision point generates an audit trail that explains the inputs and the logic used.

Sign #5: The "Key Person" Dependency

Is there one person in your organization who knows how the legacy system works? The one who wrote the code in 2005? The one who manages the root password? If they leave, the system goes dark.

Why it's a risk: This is a massive Continuity of Operations (COOP) risk. If that person is sick, quits, or retires, your ability to maintain the system collapses. From a compliance standpoint, this violates the principle of Segregation of Duties and creates a single point of failure that auditors hate. It also means you cannot implement new security controls because no one knows how to do it safely.

The Fix: Knowledge transfer programs. Document processes. Modernize the codebase so it uses standard, well-documented patterns that any qualified developer can understand.

What Happens If You Ignore These Signs?

The timeline usually looks like this:

  1. The Audit: An OIG or 3PAO identifies the gaps. You receive a list of POA&Ms (Plans of Action and Milestones).
  2. The Freeze: Leadership panics. They halt new development to focus entirely on "fixing compliance."
  3. The Cost Spike: You spend emergency funds on consultants to write documentation instead of building features.
  4. The Shutdown: If the risks are severe enough, the Authorizing Official revokes the ATO. The system goes offline. Mission disruption occurs.

This cycle is predictable. But it is avoidable.

The Path Forward: Governed Modernization

You don't have to wait for the audit to fix these problems. The most successful agencies treat these five signs as early warning signals and take action before the risk escalates.

The solution isn't just to "patch" the system. It is to apply a Governed Modernization Strategy:

  • Replace manual processes with automated pipelines.
  • Consolidate data into secure, governed lakes.
  • Map and document every dependency.
  • Refactor logic for explainability.
  • Democratize knowledge so the system doesn't rely on one person.

This approach turns your legacy system from a liability into an asset that passes audits with flying colors.

The Bottom Line

Your legacy system isn't just old code. It's a reflection of your risk posture. If you see these five signs, don't wait for the OIG to point them out. Take control of your compliance narrative today.
