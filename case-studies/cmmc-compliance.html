Achieving CMMC Level 2 Certification for a Top-15 Defense Prime Contractor

From 47 non-compliant controls to full certification in 11 months — securing a $45M contract renewal.

Executive Summary

A top-15 DoD prime contractor with annual revenue exceeding $2B faced a critical deadline: achieve CMMC Level 2 certification within 18 months of contract award or forfeit eligibility for a $45M program renewal. An initial gap assessment revealed 47 NIST 800-171 controls rated as non-compliant across security, incident response, and supply chain risk management domains. New South Technologies led a targeted remediation campaign that addressed all deficiencies, prepared documentation for third-party assessment, and secured CMMC Level 2 certification three months ahead of schedule. The contractor retained the contract and positioned itself competitively for future DoD opportunities requiring Protected Critical Infrastructure Information (PCII).

The Challenge

⏰

Contractual Deadline

A multi-year contract renewal valued at $45M included a CMMC Level 2 requirement as a condition of award. The 18-month clock started at contract signing, with certification required before the first milestone payment could be released. Failure meant automatic disqualification and potential cascading effects on 3 related programs totaling $120M in revenue.

📊

Compliance Gap Severity

An internal self-assessment using NIST SP 800-171A identified 47 control deficiencies spanning 6 control families including Access Control, Audit & Accountability, Configuration Management, Incident Response, Risk Assessment, and Supply Chain Risk Management. Primary issues ranged from shared accounts and missing MFA to incomplete logging and no formal incident response plans.

👥

Cultural and Resource Constraints

Security was viewed as an IT overhead function, not a business enabler. The security team consisted of 4 FTEs supporting enterprise IT of 120+ staff. Budget for compliance tooling had been frozen for 2 years. Most engineers were unaware that their systems handled Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), leading to ad-hoc protection measures that varied wildly by facility.

The Approach

01

Gap Validation and Roadmap Design

Months 1–2

New South Technologies began with an independent validation of the contractor's self-assessment. Using automated scanning tools and manual interviews, the team confirmed 43 of 47 identified gaps and uncovered 4 additional deficiencies in network segmentation and encryption key management.

The team segmented remediation work into three tracks:

  • Quick wins — policy updates, documentation fixes, low-effort technical configurations (estimated 3 weeks)
  • Medium effort — tool deployments, process changes, training rollouts (estimated 2–3 months)
  • Complex initiatives — infrastructure rearchitecture, SIEM implementation, supply chain assessments (estimated 4–6 months)
Key decision: The team chose to implement Microsoft 365 Defender as the foundational security stack rather than purchasing point solutions. This leveraged existing licenses while providing unified threat detection, email security, and endpoint protection aligned with NIST 800-171 requirements.
02

Policy Framework and Awareness Foundation

Months 3–4

Before touching technical controls, the team established the policy foundation required for CMMC documentation:

  • Updated 23 policies covering access control, incident response, configuration management, and risk assessment
  • Created 14 standard operating procedures (SOPs) for day-to-day security operations
  • Developed a 90-day CUI awareness training curriculum rolled out to all 3,500 employees
  • Established a CUI marking and handling guide specific to the contractor's document management systems

Early win: The revised Access Control policy eliminated shared administrative accounts within 30 days. This single change satisfied 5 of 8 non-compliant AC controls.

03

Technical Remediation and Tool Deployment

Months 5–8

This phase concentrated on the highest-effort control families: Audit & Accountability, Configuration Management, and Incident Response.

SIEM Implementation

The team deployed Microsoft Sentinel as a cloud-native SIEM, ingesting logs from 147 endpoints, 34 servers (including 12 hosting CUI), network firewalls and switches at 6 facilities, and email gateway/cloud application activity. Log retention was configured at 1 year to meet CMMC archival requirements.

Configuration Baselines

Using Microsoft Endpoint Configuration Manager, the team pushed hardened baseline configurations across 89% of the IT estate. The remaining 11% consisted of legacy engineering workstations that were isolated on a segregated VLAN with strict egress filtering and manual patching schedules.

Incident Response Overhaul

The team drafted an Incident Response Plan aligned with NIST SP 800-61 Rev. 2, then conducted a full tabletop exercise simulating a ransomware incident affecting CUI systems. The exercise revealed communication delays and incomplete vendor contacts, corrected in a second tabletop 4 weeks later.

Result of Phase 3: 38 of 47 controls remediated; 9 remaining controls required vendor assessments or minor configuration tweaks.

04

Third-Party Assessment Preparation

Months 9–11

With remediation substantially complete, the team shifted to audit preparation:

  • Compiled a System Security Plan (SSP) documenting all 110 NIST 800-171 controls and their implementation status
  • Created a Plans of Action & Milestones (POA&M) for the 3 controls with partial implementation
  • Engaged a CMMC Accredited Third-Party Assessment Organization (C3PAO) and coordinated a pre-assessment review
  • Conducted a mock audit with 12 randomly selected employees testing their knowledge of CUI handling and incident reporting procedures
Official assessment: The CMMC Level 2 assessment occurred over 6 days in Month 11. The assessor sampled 47 controls across all 14 control families and reviewed evidence from 120 employees and 34 systems. Result: Full certification granted.

What Made This Different

Business leadership drove compliance, not IT

By elevating the program to the CFO and Business Development leadership (who controlled contract eligibility), we ensured security wasn't treated as an IT overhead problem. When engineers resisted MFA rollout due to workflow concerns, business unit leaders authorized temporary compensating controls while permanent solutions were deployed.

Automation replaced manual compliance tracking

The SIEM and endpoint management tooling generated compliance evidence continuously rather than requiring quarterly spreadsheets. This reduced post-certification maintenance from an estimated 40 hours/week to less than 15 hours/week for the internal security team.

The program was scoped to competitive advantage

Beyond achieving certification, the team documented the contractor's "compliance maturity" as a business development asset. The CISO now includes CMMC Level 2 status in proposals for 6 additional programs totaling $85M in potential revenue.
