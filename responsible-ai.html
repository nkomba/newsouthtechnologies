AI you can defend to an Inspector General

The federal bar for AI has shifted from "does it work?" to "can you prove it is fair, governed, and unbiased?" Two things drive that, and they are not the same. The NIST AI Risk Management Framework shapes how agencies expect models to be explained, tested for bias, and monitored after deployment. OMB M-26-04 is narrower and specific: the large language models the government procures must be truth-seeking and ideologically neutral.

We build AI programs that satisfy both — the broad governance discipline and the unbiased-LLM procurement bar — with governance designed in, not retrofitted after a finding.

Our approach

  • Data governance first. Trustworthy AI starts with trustworthy data. We establish lineage, quality controls, access policies, and retention rules so models are trained and run on data you can account for.
  • Explainability (XAI). We implement techniques that make model decisions interpretable to program staff, auditors, and affected members of the public — not just data scientists.
  • Bias measurement & mitigation. We test models for disparate impact across protected groups, document the results, and apply mitigation before deployment.
  • Model risk management. Every model gets a risk tier, documented intended use, and guardrails proportional to its impact.
  • Continuous monitoring. Post-deployment, we watch for drift, degradation, and emerging bias, with alerting and a documented response path.

What you get

  • An AI governance framework built on the NIST AI RMF, with readiness for OMB M-26-04 unbiased-LLM procurement
  • Model documentation and explainability that survives audit scrutiny
  • Bias audits with documented methodology and mitigation
  • A data governance program — lineage, quality, access, and retention
  • Continuous monitoring for drift, degradation, and fairness
  • Accessible AI interfaces conformant with Section 508 / WCAG 2.1 AA

Standards & compliance alignment

  • OMB M-26-04 (Unbiased AI Principles) — requires large language models the government procures to be truth-seeking and ideologically neutral
  • NIST AI Risk Management Framework — the basis for our explainability, bias-testing, and monitoring practices; govern, map, measure, and manage AI risk
  • NIST SP 800-53 & Privacy Framework — security and privacy controls for AI systems and their data
  • Section 508 / WCAG 2.1 AA — accessible AI-driven interfaces and outputs

Frequently asked questions

What does OMB M-26-04 actually require?

It is narrower than most people assume. M-26-04 governs the procurement of large language models: LLMs the government buys must be truth-seeking and ideologically neutral rather than skewed by an undisclosed viewpoint. The broader expectations often lumped in with it — explainability, bias testing across protected groups, and post-deployment monitoring — come from the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and audit/OIG scrutiny, not from M-26-04 itself. We help you meet both: the unbiased-LLM procurement bar and the wider governance discipline.

Can you make an existing model explainable, or do we have to start over?

Often we can add explainability and monitoring around a model you already have. Where a model can't be made accountable, we'll tell you plainly and recommend a path that can.

Why does data governance come before the AI work?

A model is only as trustworthy as the data behind it. Without lineage, quality controls, and access policies, you can't defend the model's outputs — so we establish that foundation first.
