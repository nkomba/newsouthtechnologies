## 1. Top 3 emerging compliance requirements (AI + Cloud, 2026)

These are framed for federal contractors doing AI-enabled legacy modernization and cloud migration.

1. **Responsible / “Unbiased” AI governance baked into contracts**  
   - OMB’s 2025–26 guidance on Unbiased AI Principles requires agencies to ensure procured LLMs and AI systems are neutral, non‑partisan, explainable, and free from encoded ideological bias. [energy](https://www.energy.gov/sites/default/files/2026-05/AL%202026-05%20Unbiased%20AI%20Principles%20M-26-04_5.8.26.pdf)
   - GSA’s draft GSAR 552.239‑7001 AI clause (Refresh 31) would force contractors to demonstrate that AI systems are secure, unbiased, explainable, and aligned with federal AI governance policies, with documentation of training data, risk assessment, and model behavior. [cap50](https://cap50.com/blog/how-is-gsa-changing-ai-procurement-in-2026-what-contractors-need-to-know-before-selling-ai-to-the-government/)
   - Agencies are converging around NIST’s AI Risk Management Framework, demanding evidence of bias testing, explainability, audit trails, and continuous monitoring for AI systems used in mission workflows. [ogletree](https://ogletree.com/insights-resources/blog-posts/federal-agencies-roll-out-ai-strategy-plans-takeaways-for-government-contractors/)

2. **Expanded AI disclosure, data rights, and use restrictions in federal contracts**  
   - The proposed GSA AI clause would require contractors to disclose *all* AI systems used in contract performance (not just those sold to government), and whether they were modified to comply with any non‑U.S. regulatory frameworks. [wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-GSA-Delays-Rollout-of-AI-Procurement-Terms-and-Conditions-Extends-Comment-Period-to-April-3)
   - The government claims very broad rights over “Government Data” and “Custom Developments” generated under AI contracts, including prohibitions on using government data to train or improve models, and requirements for “eyes‑off” handling, data localization, and logical segregation of government data. [wiley](https://www.wiley.law/alert-GSA-Delays-Rollout-of-AI-Procurement-Terms-and-Conditions-Extends-Comment-Period-to-April-3)
   - When agencies procure LLMs, they must obtain model and data cards, acceptable‑use policies, and detailed information about pre‑training and fine‑tuning to ensure compliance with Unbiased AI Principles and transparency thresholds. [whitehouse](https://www.whitehouse.gov/wp-content/uploads/2025/12/M-26-04-Increasing-Public-Trust-in-Artificial-Intelligence-Through-Unbiased-AI-Principles-1.pdf)

3. **Tightened FedRAMP / CMMC / Zero Trust expectations for cloud migrations**  
   - FedRAMP is emphasizing automation, reuse of authorizations, and faster but more rigorous reviews; cloud offerings used in modernization are expected to be FedRAMP‑authorized or on a clear path. [youtube](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kYQrJ9UDpL8)
   - CMMC 2.0 implementation (and associated NIST 800‑171 controls) now requires every system handling FCI/CUI to maintain a current CMMC status in SPRS, with robust CUI policies, incident‑response protocols, and 8‑hour incident reporting expectations. [whitecase](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/2025-upheaval-2026-strategy-key-regulatory-risks-and-opportunities-government)
   - DoD and civilian buyers are tying cloud modernization to Zero Trust, supply‑chain risk management, data provenance, and bans/restrictions on certain foreign software, which affects architecture choices, AI tool selection, and hosting regions (e.g., GCC High). [ecfdata](https://www.ecfdata.com/gcc-high-modernization-why-government-contractors-are-making-the-move-before-fy26/)

If you position New South Technologies as “governed agility,” these three can become your core compliance pillars: Responsible AI, Transparent AI/Data Rights, and Secure Cloud/Zero Trust Modernization.
