The problem with rip-and-replace

Most federal modernization programs fail not because the target architecture is wrong, but because the transition is too abrupt. A full rewrite freezes feature delivery for years, concentrates risk into a single cutover weekend, and leaves auditors with no continuous evidence trail. Meanwhile the mainframe keeps running — and keeps accruing risk.

We take the opposite approach. We decouple business logic from legacy systems incrementally, so value ships continuously and every step is reversible, observable, and compliant.

Our approach

Our modernization method is built around the strangler-fig pattern: new governed services gradually take over responsibilities from the legacy system until it can be safely retired.

  • Assess & map. We inventory the legacy estate, map data flows and business logic, and rank candidates by risk, dependency, and mission value.
  • Establish a compliant landing zone. Every migration begins with a FedRAMP-aligned cloud landing zone — identity, logging, encryption, and network controls baked in from day one.
  • Strangle incrementally. We stand up modern services behind a routing facade and redirect traffic capability by capability, so the legacy system shrinks while the new one proves itself in production.
  • Prove compliance continuously. Each increment produces traceable evidence — control mappings, test results, and change records your auditors and Inspectors General can follow.
  • Decommission with confidence. Only when a capability is fully replaced and verified do we retire the corresponding legacy component.

What you get

  • A FedRAMP-aligned landing zone and containerized, cloud-native services

Standards & compliance alignment

Our modernization work is designed to satisfy the frameworks federal programs are measured against:

  • FedRAMP — cloud landing zones and services aligned to authorization requirements
  • NIST SP 800-53 — security and privacy controls mapped to each increment
  • Section 508 / WCAG 2.1 AA — accessible interfaces for every modernized application
  • Cloud Smart & TMF priorities — incremental delivery that fits federal funding and oversight models

Frequently asked questions

Do we have to take the system offline during migration?

No. The strangler-fig approach runs the legacy and modern systems in parallel and shifts traffic incrementally, so there is no scheduled outage or mission freeze.

How do you keep auditors satisfied mid-migration?

Every increment generates control mappings, automated test evidence, and change records. Your compliance posture is continuous, so an audit at any point in the program has a complete trail.

What happens to our institutional business logic locked in COBOL?

We extract and document that logic as part of the assessment phase, re-implementing it as tested, version-controlled services rather than a black box only a few people understand.
