1. Executive Summary

New South Technologies is committed to delivering excellence in legacy modernization, governed agility, cybersecurity, and AI governance services. This Customer Resolution Process establishes a clear, accountable framework for addressing client concerns, complaints, or service issues. Our approach integrates Six Sigma quality management principles with agile responsiveness to ensure timely, transparent, and satisfactory resolutions.

Every concern received is treated as an opportunity to strengthen our partnership and improve our delivery practices.

2. Purpose and Scope

2.1 Purpose

To provide a standardized, fair, and efficient process for receiving, evaluating, and resolving customer complaints or issues in a manner that preserves trust and drives continuous improvement.

2.2 Scope

Applies to all clients, prospective clients, and stakeholders engaging with New South Technologies across all service lines:

  • Legacy Modernization
  • Governed Agility Framework
  • Cybersecurity & Compliance
  • AI Governance
  • Executive Coaching & Organizational Development

This process covers concerns related to service delivery, deliverable quality, communications, timelines, billing, and contractual obligations.

8. Detailed Resolution Workflow

Step 1: Receipt and Logging

  • All concerns entered into centralized tracking system
  • Unique case ID assigned (format: CRS-YYYY-NNNN)
  • Initial categorization applied (service line, concern type)
  • Automated receipt notification sent to submitting party

Duration: Immediate upon submission  |  Output: Logged case with tracking number

Step 2: Acknowledgment

  • Formal acknowledgment sent within SLA timeframe (Section 5)
  • Case ID provided to client
  • Expected timeline communicated based on severity
  • Case owner identified and introduced

Duration: 4–48 business hours (per severity)  |  Output: Acknowledgment confirmation

Step 3: Investigation

  • Facts gathered from all relevant parties (internal and external)
  • Documentation reviewed (deliverables, emails, contracts, meeting notes)
  • Root cause preliminary assessment conducted
  • Impact evaluation completed (timeline, cost, reputation, compliance)
  • Stakeholders identified and briefed as appropriate

Duration: Typically 1–5 business days  |  Output: Investigation findings summary

Step 4: Resolution Development

  • Remediation options developed (often 2–3 alternatives)
  • Cost/benefit analysis performed where applicable
  • Timeline for each option estimated
  • Client preference solicited and incorporated
  • Agreement on path forward documented

Duration: 1–3 business days after investigation  |  Output: Resolution proposal with sign-off

Step 5: Implementation

  • Resolution executed per agreed approach
  • Verification testing performed (if applicable)
  • Quality checks completed
  • Client confirmation obtained
  • Documentation updated in case file

Duration: Varies by complexity (1–10 business days)  |  Output: Implemented resolution with verification

Step 6: Closure and Learning

  • Customer satisfaction survey administered
  • Root cause analysis finalized (mandatory for P1–P2)
  • Corrective and preventive actions (CAPA) documented
  • Process improvements initiated if systemic issue identified
  • Case archived in tracking system

Duration: Within 3 business days of resolution  |  Output: Closed case record, improvement initiative

9.2 Triggers for Escalation

An escalation is warranted when any of the following occur:

  • SLA breach risk identified
  • Client requests escalation explicitly
  • Cross-functional dependency blocking resolution
  • Reputational risk assessment requires leadership attention
  • Contractual or legal implications arise
  • Pattern of repeated issues with same client

9.3 External Communication Protocol

  • All public-facing communications pre-approved by leadership
  • Legal review engaged for contractual or regulatory implications
  • Media/press inquiries directed to designated spokesperson only
  • No unauthorized disclosures to third parties

12. Confidentiality and Data Protection

12.1 Information Handling

  • All customer concerns handled with strict confidentiality
  • Personal identifiable information (PII) protected under applicable privacy policies
  • Case records accessible only to need-to-know personnel
  • Encryption applied to electronic case files where appropriate

12.2 Record Retention

  • Case records retained for minimum 7 years per professional liability requirements
  • Archival and retrieval procedures in place for audit purposes
  • Secure destruction protocol after retention period expires

12.3 Regulatory Alignment

This process aligns with relevant frameworks including:

  • Federal Acquisition Regulation (FAR) provisions for contractor conduct
  • CMMC 2.0 requirements for controlled unclassified information (CUI)
  • GDPR and state privacy laws where applicable
  • Industry-specific compliance standards (e.g., HIPAA, FedRAMP)
